## THE WOUND TRANSLATIONS

### Fear (Vyana Vayu — chest, breath, peripheral awareness)

**Framework:** Vyana Vayu contracts. Prana stops circulating freely and pulls toward the core.

**Gen Z:** That tightness in your chest when you try to be bigger than the space you were allowed to take up. The breath that stops halfway. The shrinking you do automatically when someone looks at you too closely.

**Hook:** "Your chest knows something your brain won't admit."

---

### Shame (Apana Vayu — belly, base, gut)

**Framework:** Apana reversal. Energy that should move freely downward gets blocked and reversed upward.

**Gen Z:** The weight in your gut that showed up before you had words for it. The cringe you feel about wanting things — that's not you. That's your parents' fear of wanting, living in your nervous system. You absorbed it as fact. It was never yours.

**Hook:** "That gut feeling that something's wrong with you? It's not yours. It never was."

---

### Guilt (Samana Vayu — solar plexus, diaphragm)

**Framework:** Samana blockage. Undigested experience held at the solar plexus, repeatedly activated, never metabolised.

**Gen Z:** The thing you keep swallowing but can't digest. Every experience you pushed down instead of processing. It's sitting right there — solar plexus, diaphragm — the emotional backlog your body has been holding because your mind refused to feel it.

**Hook:** "You didn't process it. Your body is still holding the receipt."

---

### Unworthiness (Prana Vayu — heart, lungs, throat)

**Framework:** Prana Vayu restriction. Inability to receive what is genuinely offered.

**Gen Z:** Why you can't receive a compliment without deflecting. Why love feels suspicious. Why every good thing comes with the thought "I don't deserve this." The receiving channel got blocked before you knew you had one.

**Hook:** "You're not bad at receiving love. You were taught it wasn't safe."

---

### Suppression (Udana Vayu — throat, head)

**Framework:** Udana blockage. Genuine knowing and expression held back by fear of consequences.

**Gen Z:** Everything you swallowed instead of said. Every truth you edited before it left your mouth. The real you, stuck in draft mode. Your throat knows exactly what needs to be said — your survival brain keeps hitting backspace.

**Hook:** "The real you has been in draft mode your entire life."

## THE TWO PRESENTATIONS — TRANSLATED

### Presentation One: The Pure Mind → "The Overthinker"

**Framework voice:** The person who has optimised everything reachable by intelligence and finds that the optimisation produces diminishing returns on the one thing they actually want: to feel alive.

**Gen Z voice:** You've read the books. Done the therapy. Watched the videos. You can explain your attachment style, name your trauma responses, map your childhood wounds with clinical precision. You understand *everything* about yourself.

And nothing has changed.

Because understanding was never the problem. You've been debugging the wrong layer. The pattern isn't in your head — it's in your body. In the layer your intellect can't reach. The one you've been skipping.

**The line that hits:** "You can explain your patterns perfectly. You can't stop them. That gap? That's where we work."

---

### Presentation Two: The Body-But-Wound → "The Disciplined One"

**Framework voice:** The person who found the body but brought the wound with them. Five years of gym. Breathwork retreats. The inner child wound did not dissolve. It became more disciplined.

**Gen Z voice:** You found the body. Cold plunges. Breathwork. Gym at 5am. You *look* like someone who's done the work. But the anxiety is still there. The shame just has better posture now.

Because the practice never reached where the pattern actually lives. You went to the body with the mind's agenda — to fix, to optimise, to control. The layer underneath — where the wound actually is — needs something your discipline can't give it: softness.

**The line that hits:** "You've been in the body for years. The conditioning is still running. Not because the practice failed — because it never reached where the conditioning lives."

## VISUAL SITE PRINCIPLES

### What the Gen Z visual experience must feel like:

1. **Scroll = descent.** Every scroll takes them deeper, not forward. Down the page = down through the layers.
2. **Dark + warm.** Not tech-dark. Not wellness-light. Think: 2am revelation. The phone in the dark when something finally clicks.
3. **Text as event.** Words appear, build, reveal. Not sitting there waiting. Each line *arrives*.
4. **The body as map.** Visual descent through an abstract body/form. Layers dissolving as you scroll.
5. **No clutter.** Massive whitespace (darkspace). One idea per screen. Let it breathe.
6. **Micro-interactions everywhere.** Hover reveals. Scroll-triggered text. Parallax layers. The page feels alive.
7. **Mobile-first.** This audience is 90% mobile. Design for the thumb, test on desktop.

### Animation philosophy:

- **Entrance = emergence.** Things come into being, not onto screen. Fade-from-deep, not slide-from-side.
- **Text reveals are slow.** Word by word or line by line. Give weight to language.
- **Transitions between sections feel like descent.** Dissolve, sink, settle — never snap or bounce.
- **Nothing loops.** Everything happens once, with intention. No spinning, pulsing, or decorative motion.
- **The scroll position IS the journey.** Progress indicator as descent meter, not page percentage.

### Tech stack for /template:

- HTML/CSS/JS (no framework — keeps deploy simple)
- GSAP + ScrollTrigger (CDN) for scroll-driven animations
- CSS custom properties for the design system
- Canvas for particle/grain effects
- SVG for the body/layer diagrams
- Responsive from mobile-first
